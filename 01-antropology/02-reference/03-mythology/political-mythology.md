# Political Mythology

## Overview
> Mythology is never a passive collection of ancient fables; it is an active, weaponized instrument of statecraft, psychological warfare, and military mobilization used to justify violence and manipulate populations.

## Core Concepts
- **The "Second Reconquista":** Nationalist Spain under General Francisco Franco resurrected the medieval Reconquista to cast their civil war as a holy crusade. This mythic framing was reinforced through state rituals and allowed the regime to abstract atrocities, reducing political slaughter to a spiritual duty.
- **The Martyrdom Myth:** Politicized networks aggressively embellish military events to construct a myth of heroic martyrdom. For example, the Nationalist defense of the Alcázar was elevated to a sacred myth using biblical and historical parallelism, despite the reality being far less glamorous.
- **Foundational Myths of Defeat:** The anti-Bolshevik White armies utilized the grueling "Ice March" of the Russian Civil War as a legendary saga.In the absence of administrative competence, this epic became the primary ideological glue for the White movement.
- **Left-Wing and Soviet Myth-Making:** The Soviet state and Comintern used cinematic rewriting and heroic legends to enforce military discipline and party authority. They used legends like that of André Marty to make leadership untouchable, and films like *The Sailors of Kronstadt* to replace the historical reality of ruthlessly crushed uprisings with a myth of disciplined obedience.
- **Cold War Fabrications:** The CIA and Western psychological operations constructed elaborate myths of fictional resistance groups, like "White Hand" or "Black Eagle," to stimulate non-cooperation behind the Iron Curtain. This psychological myth-making proved tragic when Hungarian fighters relied on the false narrative of imminent Western-backed liberation during the 1956 revolution.
