# Spanish Factionalism

## Overview
> The historical trajectory of the Spanish Second Republic serves as a real-world validation of the cycle of institutional collapse, where initial grassroots idealism was systematically dismantled by intense internal tribalism and factional drama.

## Core Concepts
- **Grassroots Idealism:** The Second Republic initially emerged from a coalition of socialists, communists, and liberal republicans intending to establish fundamental rights and escape the corruption of the previous monarchical system.
- **The Transition to Factional Drama:** The new government immediately struggled with internal divisions, clashing over authoritarianism versus libertarianism, and centralism versus regionalism (e.g., Catalonia and the Basque country). 
- **Systemic Corruption and Political Purging:** As political survival superseded the public good, the state drove the centralization of power, the weaponization of government offices, and systematic violence (the "Red" and "White" terrors) to eliminate political opponents.
- **Administrative Collapse and Vulnerability:** Consumed by partisan warfare, the state became incapable of protecting its citizens, lost military cohesion against the professional Army of Africa, and rapidly degraded into a proxy battleground for foreign powers (Germany, Italy, and the USSR).
