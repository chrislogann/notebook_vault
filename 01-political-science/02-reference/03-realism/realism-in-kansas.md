# Realism In Kansas

## Overview
> Early Kansas statecraft was defined by predatory realism, characterized by total political opportunism, corporate state capture, and the rapid disintegration of grassroots democratic idealism into factional self-interest.

## Core Concepts
- **Gilded Age State Capture:** The boundary between political office and corporate enterprise dissolved, allowing state actors to serve on railroad boards and use Native American treaties to secure massive personal land grants.
- **Opportunistic Reversals:** Leaders such as General James H. Lane abandoned pro-slavery views the exact moment they recognized that leading a radical Free-State movement offered a more realistic path to political power.
- **Leverage of Famine:** Politicians like S. C. Pomeroy callously utilized the distribution of humanitarian relief during droughts as a tool to purchase political futures and systematically buy the votes of state legislators.
- **Disintegration of Democratic Idealism:** The uncorrupted farmers originally elected to frame the constitution rapidly transformed into self-interested elites, abandoning constructive governance to fight bitterly over the spoils of office.
