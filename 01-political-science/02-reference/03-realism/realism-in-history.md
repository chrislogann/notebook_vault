# Realism In History

## Overview
> Across twentieth-century conflicts, strategic realism functioned as an active, often brutal framework of statecraft, corporate survival, and personal opportunism that subordinated grassroots moral visions to the hard realities of governing

## Core Concepts
- **Geopolitical Realpolitik:** Superpowers consistently operated on the assumption that long-term strategic survival supersedes democratic solidarity, epitomized by the "Percentage Deal" dividing Eastern Europe and Western appeasement during the Spanish Civil War
- **Tactical Opportunism:** Leaders consolidate personal authority by exploiting crises and executing ideological reversals; for example, Janos Kadar betrayed the 1956 Hungarian revolution to install himself as a pragmatic Soviet puppet, minimizing bloodshed while serving Moscow
- **Systemic and Military Ruthlessness:** Realist structures enforce iron discipline and state terror to maintain control, utilizing pragmatic tools like Lenin's violent _tabula rasa_ to eliminate the possibility of returning to the past
- **Corporate State Capture:** Under realist frameworks, the boundary between political office and commercial enterprise dissolves, allowing the bureaucratic class to exploit public crises or utilize "telephone law" to secure immense material wealth
