# Hungarian Realism

## Overview
> Hungarian realism is defined by the nation's subjugation to superpower geopolitical calculations, enduring clinical territorial divisions, brutal imperial consolidation, and colonial economic extraction.

## Core Concepts
- **The Percentage Deal (1944):** Winston Churchill and Joseph Stalin clinically divided Eastern Europe into spheres of influence, disposing of millions of lives via an offhand, pragmatic transaction.
- **Soviet Imperial Realism:** Recognizing that allowing Hungary to leave the Warsaw Pact could trigger the collapse of their monolithic empire, Soviet leaders ruthlessly crushed the 1956 uprising to buy time through military consolidation.
- **Anglo-American Realpolitik:** The United States prioritized the Suez Crisis over Hungary, privately accepting the Iron Curtain and avoiding military steps while utilizing empty "liberation" rhetoric strictly as a domestic campaign tool.
- **Materialist Colonial Exploitation:** The Hungarian economy was structured entirely to serve the Soviet Union's material needs, highlighted by a highly exploitative, secret treaty granting Moscow a monopoly over uranium mining.
