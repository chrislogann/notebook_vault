# Business Intervention On Kansas

## Overview
> In the mid-19th century, the boundary between corporate enterprises and state politics in Kansas was virtually non-existent, allowing railroad corporations to exert corrupting control over early statecraft.

## Core Concepts
- **Politicians as Corporate Promoters:** High-ranking state and federal officials acted simultaneously as directors for the companies they regulated, utilizing their public offices to acquire massive personal land grants.
- **Exploitation of Treaties and Public Assets:** Railroad companies secured indirect aid by manipulating Native American treaties to purchase land at fractional costs and successfully lobbied the legislature to divert 500,000 acres of federal land originally intended for public schools.
- **Electoral Corruption:** Political campaigns and Senate elections were dictated by competing railroad factions, with figures like S. C. Pomeroy using humanitarian famine relief funds to buy the votes of state legislators.
- **Constitutional Safeguards:** Despite systemic corruption, framers of the Wyandotte Constitution explicitly prohibited the state of Kansas from directly carrying on works of public improvement, protecting the government from becoming financially liable for corporate construction debts.
