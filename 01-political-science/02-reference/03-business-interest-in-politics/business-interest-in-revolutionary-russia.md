# Business Interest In Revolutionary Russia

## Overview
> The Russian Revolution involved the violent disintegration of a deeply entrenched economic order characterized by a symbiotic, exploitative relationship between the Tsarist state and the industrial elite.

## Core Concepts
- **Urban Serfdom and the Capitalist War Narrative:** The Tsarist state unbendingly protected industrial interests at the expense of labor rights, while blatant capitalist profiteering during World War I delegitimized the regime and fueled the narrative of a corrupt imperialist war.
- **The Reactionary Pivot:** Following the February Revolution, the liberal-centrist Kadet Party shifted rightward, allying with military figures like General Kornilov in a desperate attempt to use martial law and "economic militarization" to protect their private assets.
- **Financial Warfare and Forced Nationalization:** The financial elite orchestrated a "Banking Blockade" to paralyze the new Bolshevik government; in response, the Bolsheviks utilized armed Red Guards to occupy bank premises, declaring immediate state takeover and causing total currency collapse.
- **Class Genocide and Total Expropriation:** The state formalized violence through the Cheka, utilizing dehumanizing rhetoric that labeled the business class as "parasites" to execute mass arrests, asset seizures, and the permanent destruction of the Russian capitalist structure.
