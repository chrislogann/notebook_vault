# Kansas Railroad Politics

## Overview
> Railroad corporations and their promoters exerted massive, corrupting control over early Kansas politics, actively dictating statecraft, elections, and the distribution of public lands.

## Core Concepts
- **Controlling Elections and Representation:** The fate of political candidates, including the election of Kansas's first United States Senators, was largely dictated by rival railroad factions seeking federal land grants, manipulating representation through gerrymandering.
- **Profiting from Indian Treaties:** Corporate promoters operating as public officials negotiated treaties with Native American tribes to acquire hundreds of thousands of acres at fractional prices, rewarding themselves with massive land grants for their political compliance.
- **Diverting Public School Land:** The political influence of the railroads was strong enough to override the state constitution, enabling the legislature in 1866 to divert 500,000 acres of federal land meant for common schools directly to four railroad companies.
- **Safeguards Against Complete Financial Ruin:** Despite immense corporate influence, the framers of the Wyandotte Constitution successfully protected the state from bankruptcy by explicitly prohibiting the government from becoming directly financially liable for works of public improvement.
