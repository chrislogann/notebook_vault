# Radicalization In History

## Overview
> A comparative analysis of the 1917 Russian and 1936 Spanish crises, demonstrating how military coups and the hollowing of the political center act as catalysts for radical empowerment and the rise of ideological militias.

## Core Concepts
- **Structural Instability of the Political Centre:** The collapses of the Russian Provisional Government and the Spanish Second Republic fostered a "Manichaean environment" where visceral class hatred and ideological dogma replaced the rule of law.
- **The Architects of Restoration:** The military officer corps, viewing themselves as the "spinal column" of the state, attempted to impose Castilian-style authoritarianism to halt perceived anarchy, clashing fundamentally with decentralized revolutionary realities.
- **The Faustian Pact:** Faced with imminent military collapse, centrist governments (Kerensky in Russia, Giral in Spain) armed radical left militias (Red Guards, CNT/UGT), committing institutional suicide by surrendering the state's monopoly on violence.
- **Logistical Paralysis and The Victorious Sword:** Traditional military rigidity was defeated by the decentralized sabotage of workers' councils; consequently, power transitioned permanently from the formal state to militant extremes, guaranteeing that the ultimate victor would not be a reformer, but the most radical faction.
