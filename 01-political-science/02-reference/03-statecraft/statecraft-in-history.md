# Statecraft In History

## Overview
> A historical examination of statecraft across regimes, revealing how political power merges with commercial enterprise, exercises pragmatic realpolitik, weaponizes ideology, and deploys force to govern society.

## Core Concepts
- **Integration of Capital and Public Office:** The boundary between political actors and commercial enterprise dissolves entirely; in Gilded Age Kansas, politicians served directly as railroad directors to manipulate treaties and secure corporate land speculation.
- **Realpolitik and Spheres of Influence:** International statecraft operates through cold calculations, such as the 1944 Churchill-Stalin "Percentage Deal" dividing Eastern Europe or the Eisenhower administration balancing public rhetoric against secret containment directives (NSC 174).
- **Weaponization of Ideological Abstractions:** Regimes use idealism as initial fuel before converting it into unyielding dogma; the Bolsheviks utilized the "spiritual purity" of the Cheka to depersonalize systemic execution and class warfare.
- **Violence as an Extension of Statecraft:** Coercive force is utilized consciously to reshape the social order, with Lenin treating civil war as a pragmatic tool to achieve a complete _tabula rasa_ (clean slate).
