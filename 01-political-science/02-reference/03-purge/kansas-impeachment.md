# Kansas Impeachment

## Overview
> An analysis of political purging in early Kansas, where intense factionalism, targeted impeachments, and corporate exclusion functioned as tactical maneuvers within a high-stakes spoils system.

## Core Concepts
- **Impeachment as a Political Weapon:** The 1862 impeachment trials, stemming from the "Bond Swindle," were utilized by the legislature to deliberately neutralize Governor Charles Robinson's faction and consolidate partisan power.
- **Factional Displacement:** The rivalry between the conservative Robinson faction and the radical Lane faction crippled governance, shifting political focus entirely to the spoils of office and military patronage.
- **Corporate-Driven Exclusion:** Railroad corporations systematically filtered and purged political opposition using gerrymandering and corporate dominance to dictate the location of the state capital and control resources.
- **Failure of the Fresh Start:** The Wyandotte Convention's attempt to build a government free of ambition was rapidly overwhelmed by systemic pressures, transforming idealistic farmers into self-interested political opportunists.
