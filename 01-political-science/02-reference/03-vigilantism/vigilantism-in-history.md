# Vigilantism In History

## Overview
> A comparative analysis of extralegal justice across historical periods, illustrating how the collapse of the rule of law gives rise to forms ranging from spontaneous mob fury to state-sanctioned retribution.

## Core Concepts
- **Spontaneous Mob Justice (Hungary 1956):** Enraged citizens formed spontaneous lynch mobs against secret police (AVO) after atrocities against civilians, adhering to strict symbolic codes (such as leaving "blood money" untouched).
- **Extralegal Courts and Border Warfare (Kansas 1850s-60s):** Faced with pro-slavery courts, abolitionists created extralegal tribunals and "Jayhawker" militias to protect land claims, which ultimately degenerated into full-scale border conflict and criminal opportunism.
- **Sanctified Gangsterism (Spanish Civil War):** Extrajudicial violence catalyzed the conflict (via the assassination of Calvo Sotelo) and defined the rear areas, where mobile execution squads and _checas_ executed tens of thousands in systemic ideological purges.
- **State-Sanctioned Lynch Law (Putin's Russia):** Vigilantism evolved into a top-down state tool of arbitrary retaliation, where military impunity in Chechnya and corrupt law enforcement replaced formal judicial codes with an "eye for an eye" mentality.
