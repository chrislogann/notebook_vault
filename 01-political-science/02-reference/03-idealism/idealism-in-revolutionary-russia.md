# Idealism In Revolutionary Russia

## Overview
> Idealism during the Russian Revolution spanned from the naive romanticism of the liberal provisional government to the ruthless, doctrinal purity engineered by the Bolshevik security apparatus.

## Core Concepts
- **Karamazovian Sentimentalism:** Prime Minister Georgy Lvov naively believed that declaring Russia "the freest country in the world" and releasing political prisoners would instantly elevate the morality of its citizens, assuming the essential goodness of the people.
- **Misty Romanticism:** The February Revolution generated a collective belief in universal happiness, an intoxicating idealism that evaporated rapidly against the brutal realities of winter, starvation, and political chaos.
- **Spiritual Purity of Terror:** The Cheka was built upon "idealised ruthlessness," with Feliks Dzerzhinsky seeking men of "spiritual purity" to execute a political cause elevated entirely above humane concerns.
- **Class Struggle and Sacrifice:** Ordinary Red Guards fought with the conviction of achieving universal justice over class privilege, while young anti-Bolshevik _junker_ cadets exhibited a tragic, idealistic eagerness to sacrifice their lives for their cause in a "Boys' Crusade".
