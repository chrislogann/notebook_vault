# International Business In History

## Overview
> Out-of-state and foreign business interests have historically functioned as decisive political architects and instruments of imperial extraction by bypassing state policies.

## Core Concepts
- **The Frontier of Financial Engineering in Kansas:** Out-of-state capital subsidized political operatives, managed campaigns via entities like the New England Emigrant Aid Company, exploited the state through bond arbitrage, and manipulated elections via interstate railroads.
- **Transnational Cartels and Democratic Sovereignty:** In Spain, international finance weaponized capital through credit boycotts, while corporations bypassed neutrality acts to supply the Nationalists with critical resources like oil and trucks.
- **Covert Parallel Markets:** Geopolitical conflicts create lucrative parallel markets, as seen when Hermann Göring sold weapons to both sides in Spain and holding companies disguised war profiteering.
- **Imperial Extraction:** Post-WWII Hungary suffered state-sanctioned asset-stripping by the Soviet Union through lopsided joint ventures, particularly in uranium mining.
- **Financial Warfare and Capital Flight:** Private banking cartels use institutional leverage and bribery as political weapons; conversely, predatory state environments force capital flight, as seen in modern Russia.
